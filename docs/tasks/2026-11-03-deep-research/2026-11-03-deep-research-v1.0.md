## Bottom line

- **No first-party successor fits this PC.** No official small Qwen, GLM, Gemma, Mistral, DeepSeek, Kimi, Ling, Granite or LFM coder shipped after 14 October. Qwen3.8-35B-A3B does not exist (S11). The realistic upgrades are third-party derivatives of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, plus speed work that leaves output quality unchanged. None of these models has an independent coding or instruction-following measurement yet.
- **Run first:**
  1. A read-only audit of the existing logs for forced full re-prefills.
  2. Groot-19B-A3B Q4_K_M with apps open. At 12.48 GB it is smaller than today's UD-IQ3_S and uses the same architecture.
  3. A side build of llama.cpp b12069 against the same weights.
- **Run next:** Ornith-1.5 Q4_K_M (needs an apps-closed window), MTP and draft-model speed trials, then Flint on the side build.
- **Keep for now:** production on b11562 with Qwen3.6 UD-Q4_K_M. Treat Strata as a fork experiment only. Upstream 0.2.10 still cannot run our Qwen3.6 files without the fork.
- **Do not plan around:** Flash-Next inside the 20-minute rule with apps open, KTransformers, dense 27-30B models, huge MoE models, or token-level fusion as a way to improve quality.
- **Do not use the reviewed b11669 for speculative decoding that mixes draft types.** It sits inside the CUDA corruption range b11632-b11995. The fix is in b11996 (S42). If you upgrade, skip b11669 and use b12069, or at least b12019.

### 1.3 Qwen3.8-Flash-Next

**Verified**
- **The earlier failure was memory, not tuning.** ISTA IQ3_XXS has a 47.0 GB first shard holding 43 GB of experts (expert figure from Strata's MODELS.md) (S24, S28).
  - Correction: Strata's rule is total RAM of at least the experts plus about 10 GB for the OS and other programs.
  - Current engines copy experts into RAM only when they fit with 8 GiB to spare (0.2.6).
  - With 45-50 GB used by apps, free RAM is the real limit. Strata then maps experts from the files, which its docs call "much slower" without giving a number.
- **Windows headroom.** Correction: --resident-budget-gib leaves 4 GiB free and is capped by the Windows commit limit. The 8 GiB figure applies to the automatic RAM copy (S23).
- **ISTA Coder IQ1_M** (updated 2026-10-17; re-download shard 1) [V] (S29):
  - Keeps 256 of 512 experts. 58.4 GB total: a 29.6 GB resident shard (23 GB of experts, per Strata's docs) plus a 28.8 GB n-gram shard.
  - SWE-bench Verified 79.20 vs 82.80 for BF16 (95.7%), at xhigh effort.
  - Correction: it is the smallest stock-weight standard GGUF. The third-party Swift-1.5 REAP K224 is smaller at 51.5 GiB, but its quality is unverified (S36).
- **Strata releases.** Correction: 12 releases (19 tags) from 2026-10-19 to 2026-11-02 (S17).
  - DFlash2 became the Qwen3.8 default in 0.2.10. Its Q4_0 selector takes about 625 MiB of VRAM.
  - SSD-aware chunking and GGUF prefetch are Linux only. On Windows, v0.2.5 brings 4,096-token chunks and CPU work on cached experts while the rest load.
  - The "ISTA-DAS Coder" in the 0.2.10 notes is the DeepSeek Coder, not the Flash-Next one.
- **Gyro packs** [V] (S30). Gyro-S needs 27.6 GB of VRAM plus RAM and Gyro-M 35 GB; KLD is mixed. Gyro-Coder needs agentionai's own llama.cpp build, and Strata support is only "coming".
- **Community reports on the Coder are mixed** [A] (S29). An 8 GB laptop with 32 GB RAM reports 20-29 tok/s decode. Most negative threads (Sep 30 to Oct 1) predate the 10-17 update. Thread #26 reports coding on par with IQ3_S.
- **Reasoning controls are native** (S31): enable_thinking, preserve_thinking, and reasoning_effort at xhigh (default), medium or low. There is no "high" level.

**Refuted**
- "Every published fast Flash-Next speed assumes experts in RAM" is false. lean-qwenflash streams experts from SSD at 17.6-20.9 tok/s, but on Linux with a 16 GB card and in exl3 format (S34). Neither that result nor the 8 GB laptop result transfers to this Windows PC with 10 GB VRAM and 8-17 GB of free RAM.

**Flagged**

**Reconciled**

- Base Flash-Next support (#27742, 2026-08-27) and its MTP (#29761, 2026-10-01) are both in b11562 (S32).

### 1.4 llama.cpp, b11562 to b12069

- **The GPU expert cache is already in b11562** (`--moe-cache-mib`, LRU, b11480). The SLRU policy starts exactly at b11736 [SRC] (S38).
  - SLRU breaks down when the cache holds about one token's experts: 0.6% vs 37.5% hit rate [3P, Metal, not confirmed by maintainers].
  - The fallback PR #30848 was closed unmerged, and master now refuses caches that are too small.
  - ik_llama.cpp's port gained only +1.27% on our exact GGUF [3P] (S35).
- **Pinned 4 GiB chunks arrived in b11678** (S39). The +66% decode figure is an NVIDIA engineer's, on an RTX 5090 running Flash-Next with MTP and --load-mode none. Linux was unchanged.
  - This matters only for non-mmap loads, which need commit we lack with apps open.
  - GGML_CUDA_NO_PINNED=1 opts out.
- **Whitespace mismatches force re-prefills on hybrid Qwen models** (issue #30357, open) (S40). One commenter reports up to about 28K tokens per turn, cause not established. #30557 (b11951) fixes part of it. #31380 is unrelated.
- **MTP** (S41):
  - Qwen3.6 needs the separate Unsloth MTP GGUF.
  - Correction: "gains vanish with CPU experts" is not supported. A 6 GB laptop with most experts in RAM went from 22.92 to 29.39 tok/s at n=2 (+28%) [3P informal].
  - The prefill halving was measured on the dense 27B with two GPUs; #23198 (2026-05-17) improved it by about 20% (S63).
  - Issue #23302: draft-n-max 3 changed committed tokens, so use 1-2 (S52).
- **Mixed-type speculation corruption.** Correction: CUDA only, builds b11632-b11995. The fix is in b11996, and b12006 adds a guard [SRC] (S42).
- **KV rotation constants on the GPU** (b12007). The author measured +8.7% decode on a slow host; a reviewer measured +0.9-2.4%. Expect a small gain [3P] (S43).
- **Op-offload splits on CPU layers** (#31229, open). On Vulkan this cost 16.9% for Qwen3.6. The CUDA figure was Qwen3-8B on an L4 and within noise (S44).
- **GDN chunked prefill kernel** (b11632, so in b11669 but not b11562). Corrections: it runs on compute capability 8.6, 8.9, 12.0 and 12.1, and it is not disabled under speculation. Prompt batches of 64 or more tokens still use it.
  - On an RTX 3090 it gave +6.9% pp2048 and +3.2% pp4096 [3P author] (S45).
- **MoE gate/up fusion** (#31188, 2026-10-28): +6-9% prefill on our GGUF, measured with all experts on the GPU only (S46).
- **Tool-call leak** (#30118, open). 9 of 100 Qwen3.6 tool calls lost their opener and came back as plain text. Check raw output for `<function=` before docking audit points (S47).
- **Launcher risks after v0.8.0** (S48): --model becomes required, the default port is 9931, and Windows argument handling changed (#31417).
- **Nothing new for low RAM on Windows.** No Windows out-of-core streaming exists; #31469 is an open, Linux-only RFC (S49). KTransformers is Linux-only and not GGUF-based (S51).
- **New check (S50).** PR #31028's merged tensors change only conversion/qwen4exp.py, so qwen35moe GGUFs such as Groot, Ornith and Logics should not be affected. Issue #31189 (a qwen4exp assert) is still open.

### 1. Read-only audit of re-prefills in existing logs
- **Why.** Issue #30357 says hybrid Qwen tool loops can re-process 8-28K tokens per turn (S40). The current audit runs 700-860 s against a 1200 s limit.
- **Artifact.** Existing server logs from past private and public audits (local-coding-lab/local-data and benchmark directories).
- **Method.** Count lines saying "forcing full prompt re-processing" and checkpoint restores, then total the tokens and seconds per prompt. No engine is involved.
- **Fits with apps open.** Yes.
- **Expected outcome.** Shows how much wall time is lost to re-prefills. If it is large, try a smaller --checkpoint-min-step. Each Qwen3.6 checkpoint holds about 63 MiB of recurrent state, which costs RAM. Client-side whitespace trimming is the other option.
- **Scoring.** Not scored. Any configuration change is then checked with one private-audit run: the answers should match and the wall time should drop.

### 2. Groot-19B-A3B Q4_K_M against Qwen3.6 UD-IQ3_S, apps open
- **Why.** It is the only new candidate that runs at 4-5 bit within the apps-open budget. It uses the same qwen35moe architecture and Qwen tool/thinking template, and claims 23% shorter output.
- **Artifact.** `bartowski/GrootTeam_Groot-19B-A3B-261023-GGUF`, file Q4_K_M, 12.48 GB (repo 2026-10-25, quantized with b11845). Alternatives: IQ4_XS at 10.94 GB, or Q5_K_S at 13.76 GB as a precision arm (S2).
- **Engine and flags.**
  - b11562 on port 8081: `--jinja`, `-c 65536 -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -np 1`, mmap load.
  - Size CPU-expert placement from a fresh free-RAM reading. Its experts are about half the size of Qwen3.6's, so expect fewer CPU layers.
  - Sampling: temperature 0.6, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, presence penalty 0. Thinking on, reasoning history preserved.
  - If b11562 fails to load it, fall back to b12069. #31028 does not apply here (S50), but the MTP tensor and 128-expert config are still unverified on b11562 [INF].
- **Fits with apps open.** Yes. It is smaller than the 13.7 GB IQ3_S that already runs.
- **Expected outcome.** Similar or better speed. Quality is unknown, with a real risk of regressions on instruction and citation items.
- **Scoring.**
  - Full private 36, public 60 and 12-task coding suites at 1200 s per prompt, against the UD-IQ3_S apps-open baseline and the UD-Q4_K_M 31/36 reference.
  - Report prefill, decode and total time separately.
  - Check raw output for leaked tool calls before docking points.
  - To be adopted it must at least match IQ3_S on the private audit, with no strict-TypeScript or SQLite regressions.

### 3. llama.cpp b12069 side build, same weights
- **Why.** Several small speedups with no new weights:
  - KV rotation constants on the GPU (b12007)
  - GDN prefill kernel (b11632)
  - MoE gate/up fusion (b11960 era)
  - partial parser fix (b11951)
  - mixed-spec corruption fix (b11996)

  It is also the build Flint needs (rank 7).
- **Artifact.** llama.cpp release b12069 (2026-11-03), Windows CUDA binaries, in a separate folder. Nothing in production is replaced.
- **Engine and flags.** The same mapped profile, with `--model` and `--port 8081` given explicitly. Check that the PowerShell `-ot` regex still parses after #31417 (S48). Arms:
  - (a) baseline
  - (b) `GGML_OP_OFFLOAD_MIN_BATCH=64`. This arm can also run on b11562.
